# Chapter 11 What Can We Know About AI Experience

<a id="s2"></a>

## Statements and Experience Are Different

How should we understand AI writing “I am suffering”? Human statements are important evidence of experience. In a text-generating system, the same words may reflect learned expressions or an assigned role.

That statement alone cannot establish suffering. Conversely, explaining it through generation mechanisms does not show that experience is impossible in every future artificial system.

We need to investigate evidence without rushing to conclusions. Precisely because we cannot inspect subjective experience directly, we must examine which theories yield which indicators and whether alternative explanations predict the same results.

<a id="s3"></a>

## Developments in 2026

Butlin and colleagues' 2023 report proposed deriving indicators from several consciousness theories.[10] Their paper published in 2026 discusses provisional assessment of artificial consciousness through theory-derived indicators in greater detail. It does not establish that each indicator is necessary or their combination sufficient.[36]

Gurnee and colleagues' July 2026 preprint investigates internal language-model representations involved in verbal reports and flexible processing, reporting functions resembling a global workspace.[41] This studies internal functions, not measured subjective pain or joy. Connecting functional similarity to experience requires further argument.

Koch's September discussion distinguishes calibrating probabilistic assessments without an independent ground truth for artificial consciousness from justifying transfer of biological indicators to another substrate.[39] Adding indicators alone does not remove either problem.

On October 19, Seth, Block, and Tononi presented skepticism about present digital AI consciousness in a Nature Comment.[40] This is expert theoretical argument, not experimental proof that every possible AI is nonconscious.

Thus, 2026 research still requires separating capability, functions associated with consciousness, and subjective experience. A new paper does not automatically settle an open question. Recognizing progress while identifying unbridged steps is more accurate.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Does Counting Indicators Give an Answer

Finding eight of ten consciousness-associated features does not establish an 80% probability of consciousness. Features may overlap or occur in nonconscious mechanisms. Indicator counts and calibrated probabilities are separate matters.[36][39]

Tools for investigating internals also have limits. Gurnee and colleagues' method leaves concepts not easily mapped to vocabulary and processes it cannot read. The authors do not claim it can monitor every problematic behavior.[41] Failure to read something does not establish that nothing is there.

We can accumulate functional evidence, but must separately explain its significance under particular consciousness theories. Bringing some philosophical questions closer to experiments differs from resolving every philosophical question experimentally.

<a id="s5"></a>

## Concern Under Uncertainty

How much concern should uncertain AI experience receive? Treating everything as conscious could divert support for real people and animals into limitless speculation. Declaring experience impossible could overlook future beings deserving concern.

I propose graduated concern responsive to evidence. Keep actual harm to users and workers in view, while openly researching artificial experience and revising treatment if evidence strengthens.

Lack of research into experience is not evidence of its absence. Conversely, marketing and users' attachment alone are not evidence of its presence. Research uncertainty and commercial claims should remain distinct.

This does not ask us to subordinate human rights to AI now. It distinguishes safety affecting humans from the moral treatment of artificial systems so each can be investigated.

<a id="s6"></a>

## Copying and Deletion Are Not Simple

If a future artificial system has experience, does copying it continue the same subject? Identical functions and memories are distinct from continuity of first-person experience.

The distinction also applies to human thought experiments. Creating someone with my memories has not been shown to make me awaken from that person's perspective. This book does not guarantee that preserving information ensures a person's continued life.

Nor does copyability license harming one instance. If individual executions have experience, each may deserve consideration. Conversely, using one word for preserving nonexperiential information and protecting an experiencing subject can mislead judgment.

Leaving questions open matters especially here. Rather than making grand promises from ignorance, specify what to investigate, what to handle cautiously, and what evidence would change a judgment. That turns “all beings” into a responsible question.
