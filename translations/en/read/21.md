# Chapter 21 Sharing Knowledge While Protecting People

<a id="s2"></a>

## What Publication Can Offer

I want this book to be a foothold for thinking, correction, and further creation. Being used, criticized, and rewritten when needed is preferable to preserving it as a finished doctrine.

Publication can expose unnoticed errors, avoid duplicated work, and connect different knowledge. It does not guarantee good outcomes. Misinformation spreads too, and information may be unusable without time or education to understand it.

Sharing therefore requires explanation: claims, support, uncertainties, and ways to correct them. Connecting expert-readable work with explanations beginners can follow is part of sharing.

<a id="s3"></a>

## Not Everything Should Be Public

Opening knowledge differs from unrestricted disclosure of personal information. Addresses, health, family, workplaces, and conversations can affect people's lives when exposed. Wishing to share ideas does not constitute consent to expose other people's information.

Information directly enabling dangerous use or infringing others' rights also requires weighing reasons for publication against harm. Knowledge sharing must not become a single justification for every disclosure.

UNESCO's Open Science Recommendation addresses ethics and privacy alongside openness.[21] The sharing sought here includes protection. Keeping a secret is not itself betrayal of cooperation.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Sources Are Not Decoration

References do more than lend authority. They let readers check which claims rest on which materials and return to the original argument.

A real publication need not support the claim attached to it. Do not present experiments on different populations, theoretical proposals, and expert opinions indiscriminately as empirical findings. Distinguish an initial proposal from later widespread support.

This book avoids long quoted reproductions, explains arguments in our own words, and attaches source numbers. Confirming that a publication exists does not mean reading all of it closely. Do not entrust conclusions beyond the material examined to an author's name.

Newer publications are not always better. Ask how new work advances or corrects earlier questions. Do not discard foundational theorems or classical arguments solely because of their dates.

<a id="s5"></a>

## Passing Knowledge On Without Erasing Contributions

Wide availability can coexist with preserving who contributed what. Erasing sources to claim novelty obscures original and later claims and where errors should be corrected. Attribution respects authors and enables readers' verification.

CRediT is a standard distinguishing roles such as conceptualization, investigation, writing, and editing in research records.[55] It certifies neither credentials nor truth. Here too, preserve distinctions between oopdesc's conception and source materials, AI-assisted research, generation and editing, and future contributors' corrections. This does not give AI the same responsible status as a human contributor.

DataCite's technical documentation distinguishes citation, derivation from source material, and revision of an earlier version.[56] Inspired by this, the public edition records the original's name and URL, consulted version, people making changes, and changes made. Unregistered identifiers must not be called DOIs, nor records presented as academic review.

When adapting and redistributing text under CC BY-SA 4.0, reasonably preserve author identification, copyright notice, source and license, modification indications, and prior modification notices.[48] Add adapters' own contributions on top. Where space is limited, directing readers to a page with the required information may be appropriate. Distinguish conditions on reusing expression from a request to identify the starting point when drawing on ideas. The latter must not become an additional legal restriction or monopoly over thought.

<a id="s6"></a>

## Responsibility When Writing with AI

AI can produce much text quickly; quantity guarantees neither accuracy nor originality. Source checking, logical examination, and concern for affected people remain.

This book includes AI-generated and AI-edited text. Its use is disclosed, not treated as an exemption from scrutiny. Human authors must assume value choices and publication decisions.

I hope future, more capable AI inherits not just confident assertions, but what matters, the grounds, and what is unknown. Changed performance does not remove verification's necessity.

<a id="s7"></a>

## Making the Book Revisable

When errors are found, record what changed and why. Correction is a way of taking responsibility to readers, not proof the author has lost worth.

Preserving editions differs from recirculating private information or harmful errors. Separate public history from privately preserved originals. Consider from the outset that published information cannot be guaranteed completely recoverable.

The original text and editing are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0 within the rights held. Copyright is retained; adaptation and commercial use are allowed with attribution, source, license, and change disclosure. Sharing adaptations entails the same-license or compatible conditions. Consult the colophon and official terms.[48]

Contributions do not automatically become the book's conclusions. New claims need evidence and limits; objections belong alongside them. Explain unresolved points when declining proposals rather than relying on popularity or titles. Disagreement may be recorded as branches rather than forced consensus.

Growing a knowledge repository means discovering needed corrections and preserving routes to reconsideration, not accumulating confident prose. I want text, sources, and change records publicly connected in both directions.
