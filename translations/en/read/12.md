# Chapter 12 Meaning in Life and Revising Purposes

<a id="s2"></a>

## Where Meaning Comes From

Is life meaningless unless a final cosmic purpose is proved? I do not think so. Time with someone, coming to understand something, and helping someone in difficulty can matter to those involved without an account of the whole universe.

Meaning here is not limited to commands from outside the universe. It includes significance, intelligibility of action, and participation in relationships or activities. Yet broadening the word indefinitely would let it explain anything. We must specify which meaning is being questioned.

Why the universe exists and what I will value tomorrow may be related, but are not the same question. Without an answer to the former, we can think, choose, and talk about the latter.

<a id="s3"></a>

## Periods Without a Purpose Belong to Life Too

A book proposing shared purposes must not demand a grand mission from everyone. Illness, exhaustion, or loss can make future aims hard to contemplate. Eating, resting, and being safe can come first.

A life without a clear purpose is not less valuable. Purpose is no test of worth. Concern for everyone's well-being includes support to become able to choose and room not to choose yet.

Nor is usefulness to others life's only meaning. Enjoying something, seeing beauty, or talking with loved ones need no performance report. Requiring social contribution to qualify for existence pressures those needing support.

My shared purpose creates no such qualification. It starts from reasons to protect lives despite differences in ability or achievement.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Freedom to Change Purposes

We need not obey a purpose forever once chosen. Knowledge, relationships, and personal circumstances change, and earlier aims may cease to fit.

Changing purposes does not make all effort wasted. Learning and relationships may carry into other activities. Nor must losses be forcibly redescribed as nonexistent. We can acknowledge lost time and opportunities while considering future choices.

Ask what changed: unsuitable means or an unsuitable purpose? Different interests or harsher living conditions? Separating these avoids a forced choice between abandoning everything and continuing everything.

Recognizing our freedom to change purposes requires recognizing others' freedom too. Yesterday's consent is not indefinitely today's consent. Cooperation needs review and exit.

<a id="s5"></a>

## Do Not Confine Meaning to One Equation

We can investigate meaningful activities and what correlates with that feeling. This differs from explaining all meaning through one universal equation.

Language relates not only to information but to speakers, listeners, situations, and purposes. “It's all right” may encourage or dismiss a complaint. The string alone does not determine its function.

Shannon formulated communication theory by separating semantic questions from engineering problems.[15] Its power does not make information quantity equivalent to life's meaning or ethical value.

I do not wish to expel meaning from intellectual inquiry. I want methods suited to questions: listening to experiences, interpreting language, observing conduct, and conducting surveys. Clarifying each method's scope can advance understanding.

<a id="s6"></a>

## What a Shared Purpose Provides

Considering all beings' happiness gives me an orientation for what to learn, make, and refuse. It does not supply every answer in advance.

Use that orientation to pause and ask: whose life improves, who bears burdens, can I continue now, and am I choosing means contrary to the purpose?

Meaning then need not lie only at a distant endpoint. It can inhabit learning, revision, and relationships. Not knowing ultimate truth does not make that process empty.
