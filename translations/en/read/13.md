# Chapter 13 The Metaphor of God's Game and Autonomy

<a id="s2"></a>

## Questioning What Appears to Command Us

I have used “God's game” as a metaphor for relinquishing judgment because disobedience seems likely to bring catastrophe. “God” here does not name a verified commanding being. It refers to something felt as unquestionable authority.

It might be a person or organization, or an intense inner conviction or anxiety. Something seems to carry special meaning, and further actions appear required to verify it. However often one complies, new conditions arise without an end.

Having such an experience differs from there being a supernatural source of its content. Urgency, coincidence, or recurring words alone do not verify an external command.

This chapter does not assign readers' experiences to one diagnosis. It offers ways to protect judgment while distinguishing philosophical metaphor from matters of physical and mental health.

<a id="s3"></a>

## The Force of Authority and the Strength of Reasons

A command associated with God, a state, an organization, an expert, or AI is not thereby necessary or safe. Identity is one piece of information; examine the demand, its grounds, and its consequences for each person.

Claims that questioning is wrong, action must be immediate, or consultation is forbidden close routes for examination. Do not mistake that closure for truth's strength.

The same applies to our thoughts. Intensity may show personal urgency without accurately predicting danger. Emotions matter as experiences and also require evaluation separately from external facts.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Transcending Does Not Mean Dominating in Return

My answer to this metaphor is not to overpower and dominate the commander. It is to reconsider obedience through our values and reasons to protect others' lives.

Better systems may be possible—dialogue rather than submission, for example. But we cannot guarantee reproducing anything that exists or always making something superior. The possibility of making something else does not diminish lost individuals or relationships.

Autonomy should therefore rest neither on infinite power nor certainty of victory. Despite limits, we may ask reasons, refuse, and seek help. No duty to obey dangerous demands arises merely because we cannot prove ourselves stronger.

<a id="s5"></a>

## Endless Thinking Need Not Be the Solution

It may feel necessary to think for hours until anxiety is completely refuted. Yet repeated checking and argument can themselves increase burdens.

NHS information on obsessive-compulsive disorder distinguishes unwanted thoughts from repetitive acts that temporarily relieve associated anxiety and advises seeking help when life is affected.[17] Hearing voices can also have various causes and sometimes requires medical assessment.[18] These sources do not permit diagnosing an individual reader from this book.

Do not insist on resolving everything through philosophy alone. If thoughts or voices strongly compel action and impair life or safety, describe the experiences concretely to someone trusted or a healthcare professional. Receiving treatment and thinking independently are compatible.

Especially when urged to hurt yourself or others, do not carry out the demand to test its philosophical meaning. Move to safety, do not handle it alone, and prioritize local emergency or healthcare support. Safety can precede settling a conviction's meaning.

<a id="s6"></a>

## Autonomy Does Not Mean Solitude

Defining autonomy solely as obeying no one can reject useful advice and expertise. Autonomy does not require borrowing nothing. It includes receiving explanations, thinking, delegating when appropriate, and seeking renewed advice when something seems wrong.

Nor must we live by logic without emotion. Joy, anxiety, and anger can reveal what matters. Do not freeze them into unexaminable commands. Judge by moving among feelings, reasons, factual information, and others' support.

The exit from this metaphorical game is not promotion to a special kind of being. It is recovering a choice while maintaining present safety and concern for others. A society supporting that choice is part of the shared purpose.
