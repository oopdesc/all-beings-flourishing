# Chapter 23 Objections to This Proposal

<a id="s2"></a>

## Is a Shared Purpose of Happiness Itself Domination

This objection matters. People may value truth, fidelity, or creation above happiness. Defining happiness externally and directing everyone toward it could make this book a doctrine of domination.

The proposal therefore does not align every life with pleasant feelings. It includes conditions for considering values, choosing, and objecting. If a better common expression than “happiness of all beings” is found, consider it.

What matters is not making someone's life merely material for another's plan. If our wording obstructs that principle, change the wording.

<a id="s3"></a>

## Is This Only a Restatement of Existing Ideas

Concern for suffering, fair distribution, freedom, mutual justification, and rethinking self are not this book's inventions. They have long philosophical traditions and contemporary research.

Scanlon considers what we owe each other through reasons and justification.[33] Sen reexamines information for social comparison.[25] Philosophies opening questions of personal identity already exist.[34]

I do not claim originality by ignoring predecessors and inventing names. The work here connects existing answers and open questions through concern for suffering, revisable purposes, and cosmic inquiry in a form readers can use and correct.

This connection has not been proved unique or best. Its value can also be assessed by whether readers can trace premises, find errors, and pursue further inquiry.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Is Hope for Cooperation Too Optimistic

Possible cooperation differs from inevitable universal cooperation. Harm may continue and discussion may fail. Conflicting interests, resource shortages, and unequal power cannot all be explained by insufficient dialogue.

Alongside goodwill, this book emphasizes preventing harm, limiting authority, responsibility, remedies, and exit. A structure allowing cooperators to be exploited indefinitely cannot sustain the common purpose.

Conversely, assuming everyone hostile from the outset may lose available cooperation. The 2026 international experiment's gap between actual cooperation and expectations gives material for questioning that assumption.[37] Use contextual evidence, not universal hope vested in one finding.

<a id="s5"></a>

## What About People Who Reject Reasons for Concern

My valuing fairness and concern does not ensure everyone agrees. I have no final proof compelling assent as in mathematics.

Still, cooperation may arise through other reasons: avoiding mutual harm, predictable rules, or preserving relationships. Agreement on specific conduct may be possible without identical ultimate philosophy.

Where disagreement remains, acknowledge it while restricting harm and protecting rights. Unresolved ethical foundations do not permit anything whatsoever. Discussion of social rules and their justification can continue.

<a id="s6"></a>

## Is All Beings Too Broad a Scope

Counting unfamiliar universes and innumerable future beings might paralyze present action. Imagined enormous benefits might overwhelm current rights.

This book rejects automatically placing unsupported infinite benefits above finite actual harm. Examine known lives, imminent severe harm, and enduring irreversible effects with explicit reasons.

Nor does it automatically prioritize increasing future population or total experience. Ask whose life improves and how consent and distribution are handled. Numbers of beings alone do not determine individual lives' meaning.

<a id="s7"></a>

## What If This Philosophy Is Wrong

That possibility remains. Premises may be unsuitable, broad language may obstruct judgment, or practice may increase burdens unexpectedly.

Then prioritize responding to harm and correction, rather than sacrificing people to the philosophy's reputation. Valuing a purpose differs from defending its present expression at any cost.

Using this book as a foothold includes removing weak parts and moving to another foothold, not only building upward in the same direction. Without granting that freedom, a book on self-transcendence would be inconsistent.
