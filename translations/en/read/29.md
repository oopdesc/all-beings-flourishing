# Appendix E Terms Used in This Book

**Shared purpose.** An orientation enabling people with different lives to coexist without destroying one another and address problems cooperatively. It does not align everyone with one value system.

**Happiness and well-being.** Broad terms concerning pleasant affect, life evaluation, meaning, and fulfillment. This book does not fix them to one score or one experience.

**Pain and suffering.** Discussion includes distress such as anxiety and isolation as well as bodily pain, without implying identical causes or treatments.

**Autonomy.** Participation in and reconsideration of life choices with adequate explanation and support, not independence from all assistance.

**Self-transcendence.** Revising present understanding, relationships, institutions, and purposes for better reasons, not a duty of self-erasure or constant growth.

**Intelligence.** Abilities to learn, understand, predict, solve problems, and correct mistakes, not a single numerical ranking of dignity or goodness.

**Empirical findings.** Knowledge based on observation, experiment, and surveys, with scope determined by populations, methods, and error.

**Value judgment.** Judgment about what to protect or regard as good. Factual information may revise it, but it is not identical to factual description.

**Conditional reasoning.** Showing conclusions that follow if explicit premises hold. Whether premises fit reality requires separate examination.

**Philosophical hypothesis.** A proposal about existence, subjectivity, or related matters. Distinguish observationally supported explanations from unverified imagination.

**Model.** A limited organization of selected objects and relations, not reality itself. Omissions affect applicability.

**Pareto improvement.** A change leaving everyone in the specified population at least as well off and someone better off. It depends on included people and evaluations and does not guarantee overall fairness.

**Correlation and causation.** Covariation versus changes in one factor producing changes in another. Correlation alone settles neither direction nor alternative causes.

**Conscious experience.** The subjective aspect of feeling something. Distinguish task capability, verbal self-report, and experience itself.

**AGI and ASI.** AGI denotes general AI addressing broad intellectual tasks; ASI is used for intelligence greatly exceeding human abilities. Definitions vary, and neither term by itself implies consciousness or goodness.

**Preprint.** A research document released, for example, before journal publication. It may contain valuable work; public availability alone does not establish peer review. Check later versions and publication status.

**Commentary.** Interpretation, opinion, or theoretical argument about research. Examine reasons and objections rather than treat it as equivalent to experimental reporting.

**CC BY-SA 4.0.** A public license allowing sharing and adaptation with attribution and same-license or compatible conditions when adaptations are shared. Its scope here is in the colophon.[48]

**CC0.** A mechanism for waiving held copyright and related rights where legally possible. It differs from this book's CC BY-SA 4.0 and does not apply here.[23]
