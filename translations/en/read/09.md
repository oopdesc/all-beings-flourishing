# Chapter 9 Shared Purposes and Freedom

<a id="s2"></a>

## We Need Not Choose Identical Lives

A common purpose does not require identical work, beliefs, or happiness. It means sharing conditions under which different purposes can coexist without destroying one another.

Some pursue music, family, research, or quiet everyday life. They need not be unified into one ideal biography. Shared concerns include opportunities to choose, safety, harm to others, and resource allocation.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights sets out dignity, rights, and freedoms of thought, conscience, and expression.[7] It is an important public standard for treating people, not a description of ultimate cosmic truth. Our shared purpose must likewise be evaluated by whether it expands freedom.

<a id="s3"></a>

## Can Everyone Accept One Decision Procedure

Combining several people's wishes into one social decision is difficult. Even three people ranking three options can generate a majority cycle.

One prefers A over B over C; another B over C over A; the third C over A over B. Pairwise majority votes make A beat B, B beat C, and C beat A. The order of comparison can affect the conclusion.

Arrow's social-choice research mathematically demonstrates difficulties in satisfying certain desirable aggregation conditions simultaneously.[6] It implies neither that democracy is pointless nor that dictatorship is preferable. We must identify chosen conditions and trade-offs.

Sen argues that expanding information and conditions used in comparisons changes what judgments become possible.[25] Impossibility results can open inquiry into what must change, rather than close it.

Instead of fixing one procedure for everything, combine dialogue, voting, expert evaluation, rights-based constraints, and review after trials as appropriate. More complex procedures also require accessible explanations.

<a id="s4"></a>

## Expertise and Those Affected

Evaluating a bridge's safety requires expertise. Deciding where to build it and who bears costs and noise cannot be left solely to experts. Distinguish factual assessment from allocation of benefits and burdens.

I do not think votes should mechanically receive greater weight with knowledge. The power to define knowledge can become another form of domination. Local experience and harm may not appear in examination scores.

Experts explain evidence, predictions, and limits; affected people explain what matters in their lives and what is unacceptable. The distinction is not absolute, but neither replaces the other.

Even in disagreement, protect minorities' ability to record reasons, criticize decisions, and obtain later correction. Today's majority may not remain one forever.

<a id="s5"></a>

## Does the World Need One Command Center

Does concern for all beings require one global management apparatus? I see no necessity: centralizing everything also concentrates the source from which errors and abuse spread.

Decide locally where possible and deliberate broadly on broadly consequential issues. Shared minimum protections can coexist with room for different practices. Such layered arrangements preserve learning from differences.

Decentralization does not automatically ensure fairness. Local power and discrimination also exist. Rather than answer solely through distrust of the center, examine accountability, participation, remedies, and constraints on power at every level.

A healthy society with common purposes must allow criticism of how those purposes are expressed. Obedience to an author or founder is not the measure of goodness. This book is no exception.
