# Chapter 13. Knowing the Universe and Choosing How to Live

Would understanding cosmic laws also reveal our purposes? I think the questions are deeply connected without sharing one answer. Ideals formed without knowledge of the world may harm real people. Yet describing what happens does not exhaust judgments about what matters.

We have examined scientific premises not to diminish ideals, but to avoid entrusting important ideals to mistaken descriptions of the world.

<a id="h-2"></a>

## How Facts Help Our Wishes

Suppose we want to reduce someone’s suffering. Acting requires investigating what causes it and what would change it. Strong goodwill cannot bring us closer to the desired result if we mistake the cause.

Conversely, knowing how to change something does not settle whether to do so. Does the person want it? Who bears the burden? How are others affected? Does it remove the person’s room to choose? Such questions need both observable consequences and reasons about values.

Here science and ethics meet. Ethics cannot skip checking outcomes. Science cannot escape questions about the purposes and permissible means of investigation. Clarifying their roles makes cooperation easier.

<a id="h-3"></a>

## Bringing Concern for All Beings Closer to Action

Since my previous book, I have wanted no one’s experience excluded from concern at the outset.<a id="cite-39"></a>[[39]](19_references.md#ref-39) I imagine a world in which all beings achieve their purposes and flourish. Yet those words alone do not decide what happens when purposes conflict.

If one person’s goal deprives another of freedom, both cannot simply be fulfilled unchanged. How “happiness” applies to things without experience is also unsettled. Declaring “all” does not remove problems; it calls for more careful definitions and adjustment.

For present practice, I propose this order: reduce avoidable severe suffering; protect conditions in which people can choose their lives; develop ways to resolve conflict without violence or coercion; and check whether these efforts actually help.

This is not a universal ranking derived from cosmic equations. It is my value judgment, intended to protect what is vulnerable while preserving room to discuss purposes. Priorities may conflict in particular situations and must remain revisable through reasons.

<a id="h-4"></a>

## Being Parts of the Same Universe

The matter constituting us belongs to the physical world. This does not entail that others are numerically the same subject as I am. Even without proving identity, we can recognize others as beings who are hurt, learn, and live while caring about things.

Grounding all concern in one cosmology risks losing concern’s roots if that cosmology changes. I would rather support it through several routes: understanding others’ experience, recognizing suffering’s urgency, and giving reasons for mutual respect.

Understanding the universe may widen those routes. Vast time and space may let us see present conflicts at another scale. But the universe’s size does not make a small human pain meaningless. Comparing size differs from comparing urgency for the person concerned.

<a id="h-5"></a>

## Do Not Entrust Every Judgment to a Voice or Intuition

People sometimes experience overwhelming certainty or a feeling that they must obey. An experience may be urgent without proving that an external command exists. Especially in distress, some distance between what was experienced and the interpretation assigned to it can help.

This book treats such experiences neither as proof of being chosen for cosmic secrets nor as shameful. I want to preserve a route to pause, gain calm, and check with trusted people or appropriate professionals before major decisions or risky actions based only on immediate conviction. This philosophy does not replace medical care or individual support.

Thinking for oneself need not mean carrying everything alone or discarding emotion. Emotion tells us what hurts and what we want to protect. Logic checks what follows and which actions suit our purposes. Creating conditions for judgment, including help from others, can let us hold our own rudder.

<a id="h-6"></a>

## Meaning Need Not Wait for the Final Answer

Why does anything exist? We need not postpone meaning until that question is solved. Advancing understanding a little, caring for relationships, and easing suffering include things we can choose and check now.

Purposes may be discussed and remade as well as discovered. Revising them through experience and knowledge rather than making them absolute is not purposelessness. It means caring about something while remaining open to better explanations and proposals.

I want the pursuit of truth to be one such way of living: less about owning answers than developing the ability to understand and change the world with others. The next chapters consider concrete foundations for continuing that inquiry.
