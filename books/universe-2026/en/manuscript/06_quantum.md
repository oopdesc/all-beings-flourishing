# Chapter 6. What Did Quantum Theory Change?

I do not want to diminish quantum theory’s strangeness. Intuitions developed for everyday objects do not always apply, and precise experiments have repeatedly tested that fact. Strangeness does not permit arbitrary explanations, however. Learning which explanations survive and which become difficult is far more interesting.

## Is Superposition Merely Ignorance?

Suppose a coin inside a box is heads or tails, but I do not know which until opening it. That describes my ignorance. Treating quantum superposition as exactly the same misses phenomena such as interference.

Combining amplitudes corresponding to different possibilities and deriving probabilities from them produces patterns unlike simple addition of classical probabilities. We can distinguish interpretations of why these rules hold from measurements of the patterns they predict. Successful predictions do not automatically choose every aspect of an interpretation.

Nor does this establish that nothing exists when we are not looking. Arguments depend on what “exists” means and what a quantum state describes. The mathematics of observation and a picture of the world before observation should not be collapsed into one sentence.

## What Bell’s Inequality Asked

Strong correlations between separated systems are not surprising by themselves. Separate a pair of gloves from one bag and inspecting one tells us about the other. Entanglement matters because correlations across several choices of measurement can exceed constraints satisfied by certain local hidden-variable explanations.

Bell’s 1964 paper made that constraint explicit.[11] In 2015, Hensen and colleagues used electron spins separated by 1.3 kilometers and reported a violation with a design closing the major loopholes simultaneously.[12] What is excluded is an explanation satisfying the theorem’s and experiment’s conditions together—not reality or causation in every possible sense.

The CHSH form can be seen through brief mathematics. Combining four correlations between measurements at two sites gives an absolute value no greater than 2 under suitable conditions for a local account. Certain quantum settings predict 2√2, and experiments can investigate the difference.[61] [Mathematical Note H](18_math_notes.xhtml#note-h) traces the assumptions through to the inequality.

Entanglement does not let us send a freely chosen message faster than light. Comparing records to reveal correlations differs from reading the other side’s choice using only local records. Deep correlation and freely controllable communication must be separated.

## What a 2026 Experiment Extended

Kulikov and colleagues reported an experiment producing better randomness from an imperfect random source under physical conditions. The method uses Bell tests, but not needing unconditional trust in the apparatus differs from having no assumptions. Conditions remain on separation between measurement sites and on the initial source of randomness.[13]

This cannot be read as proof of human free will or cosmic purpose. Measurement unpredictability, cryptographically useful randomness, and philosophical freedom have different definitions. It can be read as a concrete demonstration connecting quantum features to a useful function.

Scientific surprise is not only the feeling that anything has become possible. It is also discovering which conditions make something possible that was previously beyond reach.

## Does an Observer Need Human Consciousness?

The word “observer” can suggest a person noticing something. Experimental descriptions, however, concern interactions with measuring apparatus and processes that leave records. Experiments have not established that the instant a person looks at a screen must be a special physical process.

Zurek’s review of decoherence discusses how interaction with the environment suppresses observable interference in particular descriptions and leads to classical behavior.[14] This is important explanatory progress, but not a unique answer accepted by every position to every aspect of the measurement problem, including why one outcome is experienced.

Introducing mental magic because people are involved, or dismissing subjectivity because physical mechanisms are better understood, both seem premature. The relationship between producing a physical record and experiencing that record remains open to inquiry.

## What Differs Between Interpretations?

A framework that explains the same experiments can accompany different pictures of the world. Comparing them requires asking under what conditions predictions differ. Where predictions agree, experiments may not immediately choose between them.

This does not mean all interpretations or additional theories are forever equivalent. Some hypotheses modifying ordinary quantum theory predict different results. Each proposal must be examined to distinguish testable differences from different meanings assigned to the same results.

Our bodies’ matter means we are not separate from the quantum world. It does not follow that thought alone selects events or that every subject is a single subject. Acknowledging an interconnected world should not require skipping necessary arguments.
